# Big Ass Calendar Rules: Family Edition

How our household plans a year: 2 parents, 2 kids, everyone on Apple Watch.
Based on Jesse Itzler's "Big A** Calendar" approach, adapted for a family. The app enforces it in **Hearth → Big Year**.

> **Source reliability:** I could not open Itzler's own pages from this session (jesseitzler.com is blocked by the network).
> Everything marked **[Itzler]** comes from **secondary sources**: podcast recaps, newsletters and fan blogs. These are
> self-help and productivity outlets that are optimised for engagement, so they are good for the gist and weak on exact
> wording and numbers. Items marked **[Ours]** are my suggestions for this family. They are not Itzler's.
> Before quoting anything to others, verify it against his own site (`jesseitzler.com/pages/jesses-system`) or his
> podcast appearances. Sources are listed at the end.

## Part 1: The core rules

### Rule 1. See the whole year at once. **[Itzler]**
Use one view with all 365 days, not a month at a time. The physical product is a large dry-erase wall calendar.
One secondary source says it is 48 × 36 in, which I have not verified. The point is mental: when you can see the whole year, empty months are obvious.
- **In the app:** Big Year shows a 12 × 31 grid on one screen.
- **[Ours]** Also print it, or put it on a wall-mounted iPad in the kitchen. A calendar the kids walk past every day works better than one hidden in an app.

### Rule 2. Big rocks go on first. Everything else fits around them. **[Itzler]**
Schedule adventures, family time and recovery **before** work, travel, sports seasons and errands claim the space.
The widely quoted idea, "if it's not on the calendar, it's not a priority", is a **paraphrase. I cannot confirm the exact quote.**

### Rule 3. One misogi a year. **[Itzler]**
One hard challenge that defines the year. It is often described as having roughly a 50% chance of failure, and as being about proving to yourself that you follow through, not about prestige.
The misogi concept is usually credited to **Dr. Marcus Elliott** (P3 sports science). **Verify that attribution.**
- **In the app:** the "Misogi" scorecard target is 1. The Today screen counts down to it.

### Rule 4. Kevin's Rule: a mini-adventure about every 8 weeks (about 6 a year). **[Itzler]**
These are small, out-of-routine experiences (a night camping, ice fishing, a road trip to a friend's), so there is
**always something on the calendar to look forward to**. Itzler says the idea came from a friend named Kevin.
- ⚠ Sources disagree on cadence. Most say every 8 weeks (6 a year); one says quarterly (4 a year).
- **In the app:** any stretch longer than **56 days** with no mini-adventure or misogi is flagged in red. You can change the
  gap in Settings.

### Rule 5. Four winning habits, one new one each quarter. **[Itzler, weakest evidence]**
Secondary summaries pair the misogi and Kevin's Rule with adding one new habit per quarter. The "1-6-4" label
(1 misogi, 6 mini-adventures, 4 habits) is **shorthand from secondary sources, not confirmed as Itzler's own term.**
- **In the app:** "Quarterly habit" entries, target 4.

### Rule 6. Start the year light and close last year's books. **[Itzler, more recent version, unverified]**
More recent summaries (2025–2026 podcast recaps) describe a different three-step process:
1. **Get light**: clear out your physical and digital clutter. One recap includes writing thank-you notes.
2. **Close the books**: review last year. What defined it, and what would you drop?
3. **Script the year**: design its defining moments, including the misogi and the adventures.

Itzler's own site reportedly opens by asking *"what was the one major accomplishment or experience that defined last year?"* **That is paraphrased from a search snippet.** The framework seems to have changed between years. Treat both versions as valid and use the one that suits us.

## Part 2: Our family's version **[Ours]**

### What goes on the calendar, in this order (planning session, every November)
| # | Block | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Anniversary** (Me + Heather) | Set it first, every year, before anything else. Put a 2-night trip or at least a protected evening around it. |
| 2 | **Birthdays** (all four) | Mark the day plus the weekend nearest to it. The birthday person picks one activity. |
| 3 | **Family misogi** | One a year, sized to the youngest kid's ability (see guardrails below). |
| 4 | **6 mini-adventures** | Rotate who picks: Me, Heather, Kid 1, Kid 2, then two "whole family vote" slots. |
| 5 | **Parent-only misogi** (optional) | Me and/or Heather, separate from the family one. |
| 6 | **Vacations** | Prefer school breaks. Check them against sports season peaks *before* booking. |
| 7 | **Protected / recovery time** | One "nothing scheduled" weekend a month. It goes in as **Blocked** so the calendar can't fill it. |
| 8 | **Quarterly habits** | One family habit per quarter (for example, no phones at dinner in Q1). |
| 9 | Everything else | Work trips, sports tournaments, appointments. These fit into what's left. |

### Sports-season rules (GameChanger / TeamSnap)
- Once the schedule is published, subscribe to each team's feed. Hearth flags overlapping events ("who drives?").
- Before the season starts, look at the Big Year and **choose** which tournament weekends, if any, give way to a mini-adventure.
  Decide this in advance, not the week before.
- A tournament weekend away can count as a mini-adventure **only if** you add something new to it (a hike, a new town, a campsite).

### Guardrails (blind spots I see in the original advice)
- **Survivorship bias:** Itzler's examples come from someone with unusual money and schedule flexibility. A misogi doesn't
  have to be expensive. "Hard" means hard **for us**, such as a 15-mile family hike or a 24-hour no-screens camping trip.
- **Size it to the kids:** a misogi a child feels they failed can teach the opposite lesson. Define "finishing" so a kid can
  quit a section without the family failing.
- **Not everyone is motivated the same way:** streaks and rings motivate some people and stress out others. Celebrate what
  happened. Never show a "missed" streak.
- **Who carries the load:** if one parent does all the planning, it becomes that parent's job. Alternate who runs the November
  session. Each kid owns planning one mini-adventure.
- **Cost check:** the misogi and the 6 adventures get a budget line. Price them during the planning session, not afterwards.

### Calendar hygiene
- Every Big Year item is exported as `.ics` (Big Year → Export .ics) and subscribed to in Apple Calendar, so it shows on every
  paired Apple Watch with a 7-day reminder before each adventure, anniversary and birthday.
- Quarterly check-in (first Sunday of Jan / Apr / Jul / Oct, 30 minutes): is the next adventure booked? Is this quarter's habit
  holding? Any gap over 56 days?
- **Rule of one change:** once it's on the Big Year, an adventure can be **moved**, but only by putting a replacement
  date on the calendar at the same moment. Never just deleted.

## Part 3: The November planning session (90 minutes) **[Ours, adapted from Rule 6]**
1. **Close the books (20 min):** each person names last year's best day and one thing to drop.
2. **Anniversary + birthdays (10 min):** enter them in the app (they repeat every year automatically).
3. **Misogi (20 min):** pitch ideas, vote, pick a date, enter it, price it.
4. **Kevin's Rule (25 min):** fill the 6 adventure slots until the app shows **0 gaps**.
5. **Protected weekends + vacations (10 min).**
6. **Q1 habit (5 min).** Export `.ics`, subscribe, done.
